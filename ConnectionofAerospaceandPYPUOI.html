How to use this bank

The specialist and HRT columns in the Excel workbook are the taught sequence. This page (and the ideas-strands sheet) is a planning bank: extra experiments, applications and digital tasks you can drop into a 25–40 minute slot when a strand needs more depth, or when a team wants a different route into the same capstone. Filter the sheet by year, strand code, idea type, HTWW or ATL. Each idea names its HTWW connection (the line of inquiry it opens), its ATL connection (the skills students practise in the slot), and Resources needed (the packing list to run it in class). Yellow New idea rows are brainstormed here; the others extend lessons or notes you already have.

Central idea (Y4–Y6 HTWW). Understanding the fundamental laws of the universe allows humans to innovate, solve problems, and explore the world and beyond. Inquiry into patterns, cycles and systems; diverse practices, methods and tools; discovery, design, innovation, possibility and impacts.

Four aerospace lenses (non-aviation)

To connect aerospace to How the World Works without making the unit “about aeroplanes,” hold these four lenses. Each maps onto the same UOI concepts: forces in action, energy’s journeys, matter’s transformations, and global perspectives.

Capstone spine

Each year keeps the same central idea. The capstone is the aerospace application that should sit behind strand choices: weather data collected by a machine we designed.

Year 4 · Mars rover

The Chinese aerospace station wants weather on Mars (daily temperature, rainfall, snowfall, humidity). Design a rover with those sensors that can roam, with remote control.

Year 5 · CubeSat

Design a CubeSat that can describe Hong Kong’s weather (temperature, wind, humidity, rainfall) and explain fog, rain, snow, frost and hail.

Year 6 · Station arm + payload

Design a hydraulic robotic arm (levers, pulleys, gears) for the space station, plus a human-needs payload — water, air, power, safety or science for Tiangong or a Moon base.

Rover-parts conductivity tester

Compare electrical and thermal conductivity of metals and non-metals

What students do
Students build a simple bulb-and-battery tester and sort rover-part samples (copper wire, iron nail, aluminium foil, rubber tyre scrap, plastic shell, wood, graphite pencil). Record which complete a circuit and which feel warm after a short safe contact with a warm pack.
Feeds the capstone
Chooses which materials can carry power from the battery pack to the weather sensors, and which should stay as the insulating shell.
Tools
Battery, bulb, leads, mixed material samples, recording sheet
HTWW connection
The properties and uses of materials — a rover part is chosen because of what it can do with electricity or heat.
ATL connection
Thinking: Analysis — sort samples as conductor or insulator.
Research: Observing — record which parts complete a circuit and which feel warm.
Resources needed
Per pair: 1 battery pack, 1 bulb (or LED + resistor), 4 leads, sample bag (copper wire, iron nail, aluminium foil, rubber, plastic, wood, graphite pencil).
Class: recording sheets.
Shared: 1 warm pack.
Space: desks. Safety: no mains electricity.

Dust, ice and stretch — physical changes on Mars

Identify visible physical changes that do not produce new matter

What students do
Stations: (1) stretch a rubber ‘tyre’; (2) evaporate a water drop on a warm tray (Mars is dry); (3) sprinkle flour ‘dust’ on a solar-panel card then brush it off. Students argue that the material is still the same stuff.
Feeds the capstone
Dust on solar panels and ice that freezes / melts on the rover are physical changes — they must be designed for, not treated as rust.
Tools
Rubber band, tray, flour, card, brush
HTWW connection
The properties and uses of materials — matter can change shape or place and still be the same stuff.
ATL connection
Thinking: Classifying — argue that tyre, vapour and dust are still the same material.
Communication: Using the two words — physical, not chemical.
Resources needed
Three stations (rotate groups): (1) rubber bands; (2) warm tray + water dropper; (3) flour, dark card, brush.
Class: sorting cards (physical / still the same stuff).
Space: three table stations plus a sink. Prep: warm the tray. Safety: flour stays on the card, not in eyes.

Identify visible chemical changes that produce new matter (rusting, burning)

What students do

Feeds the capstone

Tools
Nails, water, salt, oil/paint, observation log
HTWW connection
The properties and uses of materials — some changes make new matter (rust, burn) and must be designed against.
ATL connection
Thinking: Classifying — sort rust / burn vs dust / ice.
Research: Observing — log nails over days in dry, wet and salty water.
Resources needed
Per group: 4 iron nails, 3 small jars (dry / tap water / salty water), oil or paint for one extra nail, observation log.

Space: windowsill for a multi-day watch. Safety: teacher-only flame; salt water labelled.
